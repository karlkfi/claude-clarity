# A selectivity rate is not a throughput prediction

A rejection rate says how often work is **skipped**. A throughput figure says
how fast the whole pipeline runs. Converting the first into the second needs the
share of total cost the skipped work carries, and nothing in `verify-claims` or
either dispatch skill says so. The closest rule,
`` `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:1358:**A figure you derived is not a figure you read.** The rule above quotes a number; this one you` ``,
governs a figure nobody measured; here the figure is real, measured, and
answering a different question than the one it is put to.

## It went wrong in both directions in one run, by both roles

Measured 2026-09-08 on `karlkfi/claude-spill-guard`, the Q164 lane and the brief
that dispatched it.

- **The row and the brief** carried a two-segment head's selectivity over
  0.46 GB — 554 bare `eyJ` keyword hits, 27 reaching a second segment past a dot
  — and framed the detection/extent split as therefore *faster*.
- **The lane** read two separate benchmark runs as a 2× regression, which
  [Q300](Q300.md) owns and measures.

The lane's own summary is the best statement of the shape: *The row and I made
the same error in opposite directions — it read a 4.9% rejection rate as a
speedup prediction, my first table read two separate runs as a 2× regression —
and only an in-process control separated either from the machine.*

**The brief had already warned that lane, in the same document.** It states
outright: *That is a rejection rate, **not a benchmark**, and treating it as one
is the first mistake available here* — two paragraphs before framing the split
as a speedup. A warning about a class does not stop the author committing it.

## What the measurement said

Neutral on the shipped ruleset. Both rulesets in one process: 37.93–39.04 →
37.55–39.58 MB/s, because `jwt` was already anchored and the other nine rules
are where the time goes. What moved is the adversarial case — 1 MiB of
back-to-back JWTs 13.8 → 56.9 MB/s at the same 6,721 findings, and 1 MiB of
`-eyJ` 16,733 ms → 2.4.

The lane refused the speed claim: *no, and I would not let the notes say it is …
What it buys is recall, for nothing.* That framing is what shipped — the change
removes a ceiling that was costing detections silently, and costs nothing.

**Do not carry the 76.61–76.76 against 76.48–76.88 figures into this row.** They
are the orchestrator's later re-measurement on a different tree, comparing
as-shipped against the intermediate bounded form, and the brief that raised this
finding conflated them with the lane's. [Q300](Q300.md) is where a control
reading belongs.

## Direction

State what a selectivity figure licenses: a claim about work avoided, never
about wall-clock, until somebody has measured the share of total cost that work
carries. The natural home is `verify-claims` §1 beside the derived-figure rules,
since it is a unit error rather than a dispatch one — but it was made by an
orchestrator writing a brief and by a lane reading a benchmark, so a clause in
the orchestration skill outside this repo, about a figure quoted into a spawn
prompt, is the alternative worth costing.

`verify-claims` is far past its ceiling and held shrink-only, which
[Q1003](Q1003.md) owns and measures; do not restate its figures here. So an
addition there names its displacement rather than raising a budget, which no
longer exists. The orchestration skill is not in this repo, so costing that
alternative means measuring it where it lives.
