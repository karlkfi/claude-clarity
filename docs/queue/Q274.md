# A cross-check built from the terms it audits cannot disagree

Rule 1 asks whether the evidence behind a claim could have shown the opposite,
and it is written about claims and their instruments. The same question applies
to a *check*, where it has a tell the skill does not name: a check assembled
from the terms it audits cannot disagree with them.

Measured 2026-09-03 across #444 and #445. A `BODY_BUDGET` entry states three
quantities per file — bytes added, bytes displaced, and the net the ceiling
moves by — and one session proposed the reconciliation `added − displaced =
after − before` as a free cross-check on any such entry.

It is a real check for #444's figures. Its displaced units were whole
paragraphs and a whole bullet, nothing reflowed, so its `added` came off the
diff independently of its `displaced`: two sources, one agreement.

It is vacuous for #445's. That session measured `displaced` from four units
individually, took `net` from the two revisions, and **derived** `added` as
their sum, because a diff counts a reflowed line as both added and removed and
so overstates displacement. The identity is therefore the definition it was
built from. It holds for any values whatever, including wrong ones.

Both sessions ran it, both got exact agreement, and nothing in the arithmetic
separates the agreement that meant something from the one that could not fail.

**The tell is that the check is an identity rather than a comparison**, which is
what makes this a test rather than advice. Ask where each term came from: if one
was computed from the others, the check has one source printed twice. A real
cross-check needs two independent routes to the same quantity — on #445 that was
the diff's own `+added −removed` against `after − before`, two readings that
could have come apart and did not.

The natural home is a clause on Rule 1 rather than a rule of its own, since it
is that rule pointed one level out. Filed rather than written directly because
`verify-claims` sat exactly at its ceiling and took +1,290 bytes on 2026-09-03;
a third same-day addition landing because the session that found it happened to
be adjacent is the ratchet rather than a decision, so this competes for the
space instead of winning on proximity.
