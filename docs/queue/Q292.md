# A number that agrees is read as corroborated, and §1 has no tell

§1 carries *A negative needs a positive control* and calls it the sharpest rule
in the file. The positive twin is not stated: **a number that agrees with one
already in the conversation has been corroborated by nothing, and reads as
corroborated precisely because it agrees.** An independent measurement landing
on the same figure is evidence; a second reading that inherits the first one's
assumption is the same reading twice, and the agreement is what stops anyone
looking.

**The word is absent, and that is not the same as the idea being absent.**
`corroborat` occurs zero times in the 94,220-byte body. Four rules already carry
pieces of the claim, which is why this is a row rather than a paragraph — the
work is deciding which of them the finding belongs to:

- `` `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:800:Two causes that predict the same count are not distinguished by that count` ``
  is the closest, and its worked example even locates the discriminator "one
  level down". It is framed as two explanations competing for one number in a
  *why* argument, and says nothing about the trigger — that the number arrived
  agreeing with a figure already held.
- `` `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:340:treat agreement between two runs sharing a setup as one reading, not two` ``
  is the same claim scoped to a shared setup.
- `` `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:684:Agreement with the enforcer is not evidence when one detail could fool both` ``
  is the same claim scoped to a hand-rolled second parser.
- `` `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:507:gets the same number, and agrees, because the agreement is about the` ``
  is the same claim scoped to a contaminated sample.

So the general rule is three-quarters written and stated nowhere. Whether that
is a gap or a file doing its job by instance is the open question.

**The instance.** Measured 2026-09-05, during an eight-PR
`karlkfi/claude-spill-guard` run under agent review. A reviewer measured a
corpus at ~3,502 MiB against a stated ~3,540 MiB and read the agreement as
corroboration. It could not be: the reviewer had capped reads at 1 MiB per file,
so its byte total was truncated by construction and landed within 1% of the
uncapped figure anyway, because few files in that corpus exceed the cap. The
byte total could not separate *the corpus is that size* from *this read is
truncated*. The file count could, and that is the number nobody compared.

This is second-hand — it is the reviewer's run as reported back, not re-derived
here, and the cap and the two figures are the reporting session's. Re-deriving
it is cheap and is the first thing to do with this row.

**A second instance was offered and does not belong to this skill.** A session
reported "nine heads" across two PRs having counted nothing; the real figure was
seven. That is a figure never read from an instrument, which
`` `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:386:A figure you derived is not a figure you read` ``
and its neighbour already own, and `claim-provenance` owns the register half.
Nothing in it is about agreement. Do not carry it into whatever this becomes —
one instance is what this row rests on.

**Measure:** whether an agreeing second figure is a recurring shape or an n=1.
A recurrence scan over session transcripts is the instrument for
this question. A single instance does not earn a paragraph in a body charged on
every call, and the four rules above mean a wrong call here adds a fifth
overlapping statement rather than a missing one.

**The body is exactly at its ceiling**, 94,220 bytes against a `BODY_BUDGET` of
94,220, so anything added is a budget raise with an accounting note or a
displacement. The displacement candidate is the honest one to test first: if
this belongs as a clause inside *Two causes that predict the same count*, it
costs tens of bytes rather than a section, and the three scoped instances above
get an owner they currently lack.
