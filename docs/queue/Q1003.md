# One body is 4.5 times its ceiling and the unused part is 19% of it

`verify-claims`'s body is **110,854 bytes** against the 24 KiB hot-tier
ceiling, measured 2026-09-18 at `1858849`. Every other skill here is between
8.4 KB and 18 KB. It is grandfathered shrink-only, so the gate is green and
nothing reports the gap — the shape working as designed.

**The residency reading is taken**, and it removes the option the row was
filed to keep open. `scripts/rule-residency.py` ranks each of the 88 rules by
how often its vocabulary reaches the output of the 85 sessions that loaded the
body. Everything with no measurable uptake — 21 rules, `BLIND` or `FLAT` —
is 20.6 kB, 19% of the file. Moving all of it leaves about 90 kB against a
24 KiB ceiling. Full reading and its bounds:
[`docs/verify-claims-residency.md`](../verify-claims-residency.md).

**Size does not track use.** The largest rule in the file is also the most
used, reaching 70 of 85 sessions; the second largest reaches 3. So a pass that
works down from the biggest sections deletes the top of the ranking, which is
why *do not treat the ceiling as the target* was right and understates it.

**What is still open** is a question the reading cannot answer, because no
transcript records which part of a resident body a turn attended to: whether
the 21 low-uptake rules are unused or merely applied in the reader's own words.
A rule reaching zero sessions is a candidate for `references/`, not a verdict.

Two next steps are available and they are different sizes. Move the 21
low-uptake rules behind a `references/` file and accept 90 kB, which is a
real cut that does not reach the ceiling. Or reassign the tier, on the argument
that a reference work with an 88-rule body is not what the hot band was sized
for — `docs/skill-body-ceilings.md` names a tier reassignment as the thing that
reverses the grandfathering, and it wants a residency figure, which now exists.
