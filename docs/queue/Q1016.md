# The residency verdict moves because the reading is being discussed

`` `scripts/rule-residency.py:303:    if row["corpus"] == 0:` `` is the whole
definition of `BLIND`, and corpus is
`` `scripts/rule-residency.py:30:    corpus  every firing anywhere, PRE and POST and authoring sessions alike.` ``.
So a rule leaves `BLIND` the first time its vocabulary appears anywhere in the
transcripts — including in the sessions deciding whether to relocate it.

**The instrument moves a rule out of the relocation candidate set precisely
because someone is discussing relocating it.** For a reading whose job is to
decide what to relocate, that is the worst available direction.

## Measured

Two readings of the **byte-identical body blob**
`584d604017ff7c9f973bfb89c6d17faca0727233`, flagged
`--repo claude-clarity --repo claude-skills`, taken by this seat:

| date | tree | blob | BLIND | FLAT | union | RAN | SAID | corpus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-18 | `1858849` | `584d604` | 14 | 7 | 21 | 53 | 14 | not recorded |
| 2026-09-19 | `6b88023` | `584d604` | 8 | 13 | 21 | 53 | 14 | 3,227 |

The body is the same blob in both, so none of the movement is the body changing.
`BLIND` fell by six, `FLAT` rose by the same six, and `RAN` and `SAID` did not
move at all — every rule that changed verdict crossed the one boundary this
defect predicts, in the one direction it predicts.

**A third reading was withdrawn from this table, and how it got here is this
row's own subject.** It was taken with the worktree on a branch in the Q146
stack, whose body is blob `c518b27`, so it was never a reading of `584d604` and
cannot serve as a control for it. Nothing in the output says which blob it read:
it returned the same five verdict figures as the row above, and the tell was a
rule's line number moving between two readings that were supposed to be of one
file. A reading taken against the wrong tree does not announce itself — it
produces plausible numbers, and here they were plausible enough to reach a
filed row and survive one review.

**The union is exact and unchanged: 21 rules, 21,120 bytes, 20.625 kB**, read
from `--json` rather than summed from the rounded per-rule column. That is the
same figure [verify-claims-residency.md](../verify-claims-residency.md) records,
to the byte.

**A second seat reproduced it independently**, three readings on its own
schedule before seeing this row: `BLIND` 14 → 8 → 8, `FLAT` 7 → 13 → 13, `RAN`
53 and `SAID` 14 unmoved throughout, union 21 rules and 21,120 bytes in all
three. Different session, different times, same numbers.

**Six rules crossed, not one.** That seat's readings 29 minutes 21 seconds apart
on one blob show six of the fourteen `BLIND` rules moving, all in the same
direction. The mechanism was found on one of them — `A skip is not a defense`,
which ran 1–17 records a day for a month and **138 on 2026-09-19**, being commit
messages, PR bodies, review comments and cross-session traffic from the round
deciding whether to fold it — but the effect was never confined to it.

| rule line | 09:21 | 09:50 | 09:53 | ~16:45 |

| 538 | 0 | 2 | 3 | 3 |

`corpus == auth` in every cell, so the reader arm is exactly zero throughout:
all six left `BLIND` on authoring traffic alone, which is the mechanism measured
per rule rather than inferred from the source. The final column is this seat's
independent confirmation some seven hours later — two rules higher, four
unchanged, none lower.

## The direction is reasoned from source, not measured

`corpus == 0` can only ever stop being true, so the movement is one-way out of
`BLIND` and never back in. **That is a reading of the source, and the tables
above do not establish it.** Five readings on this blob that never went upward
are consistent with a one-way boundary and equally with a boundary that simply
has not gone back up yet.

It holds only under a precondition worth stating, because both halves are
breakable: a **fixed blob** against an **accumulating store**. Change the body
and rules enter and leave for reasons that have nothing to do with this. Prune
or rotate the transcripts and `corpus` falls, at which point a rule can return
to `BLIND` and the direction claim is simply false.

**The per-rule table above does not settle it either, and an earlier draft of
this row said it would.** Non-decreasing `corpus` across a window of half an
hour, or of seven hours, establishes that the store did not shrink in that
window. It cannot reach the thing the precondition names as the breaker: the
store is append-only until somebody prunes or rotates it, and no observation
window that contains no pruning can tell a boundary that cannot go back up from
one that has not yet been given the chance. What the table does establish is the
*mechanism* — that the crossing runs on authoring traffic and nothing else —
which is worth more here than the direction.

Settling the direction is a question about the store's retention rather than
about the instrument: whether anything prunes `~/.claude/projects/`, on what
schedule, and what `corpus` does across one of those events.

## What this invalidates, and what it does not

**`--repo` does not protect the `BLIND` boundary.** It segregates the reader arm
only, so authoring traffic reaches `corpus` either way — and authoring traffic
is exactly what the act of deciding generates. The residency doc records the
flagged run returning its table byte for byte two hours apart and calls the bare
run the one that drifts. That stability is real over two hours and does not
survive a day of review traffic: the reading above is a flagged run.

**Any inventory count wants a stamp** — the date, the corpus size, and the
commit — the way the residency doc stamps its own reading. Every reading taken
so far has been smaller than or equal to the one before it, for reasons that
have nothing to do with the body, so a count quoted without a stamp is a figure
whose age the reader cannot recover. Whether that is decay in principle or only
in every observation to date is the question the section above leaves open.

**It does not touch the Q146 fold, and the relocation argument stands.** The
candidate set is invariant across every reading taken so far: 21 rules, symmetric
difference zero. What moves is the split inside it, which nothing should quote.

## Direction

**Ask:** which of three remedies. The defect itself is measured and not in
question; what is open is how much the instrument should give up to remove the
drift, and the options differ in what they cost the reading's credibility.

**Exclude the deciding sessions from `corpus`** the way `--repo` excludes the
authoring arm from the reader corpus. Fixes the direction, and needs a rule for
which sessions count as deciding — a question with no clean answer, since a
session that reviews a rule may also be applying it.

**Stamp and accept it**, reporting corpus size alongside every verdict and
treating `BLIND` as a floor rather than a count. Cheapest, keeps the instrument
honest, and leaves the decay in place.

**Report the union only**, since that is the figure that has held across every
reading, and drop the four-way split from anything a decision quotes. Loses the
`BLIND`/`FLAT` distinction, which nothing has yet used for a decision.

## Provenance

Three seats, independently, which is most of why it is credible. The worker
recorded the movement first, as a fact without a cause. The reviewer of the Q146
PR found the mechanism in the source and the firing count, and sharpened it to
the one-way claim. The reviewer of the Q1013 scoping pass observed the same
effect from the other side: a span reading `BLIND` in its first pass and `FLAT`
in its second and third.

The one-way claim remains reasoned from source and is not measured here; the
readings above are consistent with it and do not establish it. Two of the five
on this blob are this seat's, taken 2026-09-18 and 2026-09-19; a third of its
readings was withdrawn for having been taken against a different blob, which the
`## Measured` section records.

**One more instance of this row's own pattern, from the review of it.** The
reviewer audited this PR's citations against the Q146 branch head and got three
confident misses; that branch shifts the cited lines and is not this PR's base.
Against `main` all four resolve exactly. A citation audit run against the wrong
tree does not fail loudly — it produces specific, confident, wrong findings,
which is the failure this batch keeps meeting inside the checks built to catch
it. The reviewer reported it unprompted.

Also settled while here, so nobody re-checks it: `git grep` over the Q146 branch
head finds no claim about deslop's script count anywhere in the tree. A
four-versus-three correction raised in review lived only in the review
discussion, and nothing shipped carries the wrong number.
