# Move verify-claims incident narratives into an exhibits reference

Blocked by [Q1014](Q1014.md). Both rows relocate text out of the same body and
re-path the same citations, and a citation two open branches both move resolves
wrong whichever lands second. Q1014 is already ready and picks by a different
axis, so it goes first and this row re-derives its set against what is left.

**What a prompt audit found.** Run read-only on 2026-10-04 from a
`github-actions-gateway` session, against `f2ec632`, for a reader on Claude
Opus 5.5. Re-measured here on the same tree: the body is 1,407 lines, 19,141
words and 110,838 bytes, and carries 23 `Measured` narratives and 12 rules that
open as a delta on "the rule above". The audit's claim is that the narratives
are what makes the body long, and that the delta chains make a rule unreadable
without the one before it.

**The move, as the audit proposed it.** Keep each rule's bold sentence, its
mechanism, and one sentence of exhibit. Move the incident narrative — the date,
the repo, the counts, what went wrong in which session — into
`references/exhibits.md`, keyed by the rule's heading. While moving, re-cluster
the delta chains by principle so each rule stands on its own. Passages the audit
named:

- `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:211:passes while the probe stays broken. Measured 2026-09-01 on`
- `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:1322:answered *"i havent personally drafted any prs today. it's all you, claude."* That second `
- `skills/verify-claims/SKILL.md:1365:**A figure you derived is not a figure you read.** The rule above quotes a number; this one `

**Why this is not Q1014 under another name.** Q1014 moves whole rules ranked
low by the residency reading. This moves part of every rule, ranked by kind:
narrative versus instruction. A rule the reading ranks high keeps its narrative
under Q1014 and loses it here. The two sets overlap, so the bytes this row frees
cannot be quoted until Q1014 has landed.

**Two constraints from `decisions.md` that bind the move.**
[An exhibit may dangle; an instruction may not](../decisions.md): a narrative
that moves keeps naming its original subject, and is not re-pointed. And room is
made and spent inside one branch, so a parked clause still waiting when this row
is picked up lands in this branch or loses the room.

**Open questions for whoever picks it up.** Whether one sentence of exhibit is
enough for a rule whose mechanism was learned from the incident, which is the
case `CLAUDE.md` warns about: mechanism stripped out leaves a rule that reads as
arbitrary. And whether `references/exhibits.md` gets read at all, which Q1015
asks about relocated rules in general.

**What would reverse it:** a residency reading that shows narratives are what
sessions apply, rather than the rule sentences. Then the narrative is the
instruction and stays.
