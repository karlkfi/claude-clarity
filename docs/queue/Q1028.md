# rule-residency.py misses a rule lead that wraps

`load_rules()` takes a paragraph as a rule only where it opens with a bold span
closed on the same line:
`` `scripts/rule-residency.py:93:m = re.match(r"^\*\*(.+?)\*\*", line)` ``.
A lead that wraps puts its closing `**` on the next line, so the paragraph is
never a rule. Its text is folded into the span of the rule before it, and it
inherits that rule's verdict, with nothing in the table saying so.

**What it cost.** Q1014 moved the reading's `BLIND`/`FLAT` union out of the
`verify-claims` body, cut at `load_rules()` boundaries. *One field is a
projection of a mutable object…* (828 bytes) rode out inside *A correct reading
of the wrong field*'s span, though the reading had never measured it. The review
of PR #26 caught it and the rule went back. Anyone using the reading to rank or
move rules gets the same silent passenger.

**Its reach, carried from that review and not re-taken here:** ten wrapped leads
in the body the 2026-09-18 table was taken on, which is why that table counts 88
rules, and nine at PR #26's head `2011213` before the restore. Re-count on the
current body rather than quoting either number.

**The fix, as the review left it:** recognise a lead whose bold span closes on a
following line of the same paragraph. Confirm it with a fixture holding a wrapped
lead that the current build folds and the fix separates. Then re-take the reading
and check whether the union changes, because the passengers now get verdicts of
their own.

Not Q1013, which gates which reading a session is holding; this is what any
reading counts as a rule.

Filed 2026-10-04 from the review of PR #26.
