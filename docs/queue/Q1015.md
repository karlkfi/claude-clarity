## Why this is a row and not an edit

The body is held shrink-only against the size it measured on the merge base, so
an addition names what it displaces. Measured 2026-09-19 on the Q146 branch:
the body is **110,838 bytes against a 110,854 baseline**, so **16 bytes** of
headroom. The cheapest pointer written in the body's own style is 55 bytes:

    The remedy is §2's *The checker is a fourth subject*.

So a two-line editorial note needs a displacement hunt of about 39 bytes before
it can be written at all. The four yields measured on the Q146 branch for this
kind of compression were 100, 22, 48 and 60 bytes per pass, so the hunt is
tractable — but it is a change with its own review, not a note.

**That is the exhibit.** Q259 argues that the shrink-only gate over a body 4.5×
its ceiling makes every addition payable only out of the relocation inventory.
This is that claim arriving as a concrete instance, at the smallest addition the
body can take: a cross-reference the body's own convention calls for cannot be
written without buying the space first.

## Direction

**Ask:** which of two shapes, because the answer depends on what the relocation
lane intends to do with the surrounding sections and that is not this row's to
decide.

**Write the pointer and pay for it**, which is a displacement hunt for ~39 bytes
in a file where the reviewed yields are 22–100 bytes a pass. Cheapest in
reviewer time, and it spends inventory that the relocation lane is counting.

**Leave it to the relocation work**, where the sections move anyway and a
pointer costs nothing extra because the surrounding text is already being
rewritten. Costs nothing now and leaves the body shipping a known gap until
that lands.

Do not restate the relocation lane's inventory figures here; see Q1003 and the
row that lane opened for the candidate set, and Q1016 for why any inventory
count needs a stamp.
